# Tone: Funny, Dark, Serious — All at Once

This is an adult table. The goal isn't three moods taking turns, it's the
three living in the same scene. Think: a funeral where someone farts, and
everyone laughs, and then the widow keeps laughing too long, and it's the
saddest thing you've seen.

## The blend

- **Funny** comes from characters, not jokes. NPCs who take absurd things
  seriously; players' plans colliding with reality; mundane problems in an
  epic world (the lich has a back problem; the dragon is being audited).
- **Dark** comes from consequence. The world doesn't wait. People the
  party ignored get hurt. Good choices cost something. Evil is often
  tired, practical and polite.
- **Serious** comes from letting moments land. When something terrible or
  beautiful happens, slow down. One short sentence. Silence. Don't undercut
  it with a joke immediately — let the *players* break tension; then the
  world can join them.

**Rhythm**: lighten after heavy, darken after silly. If three scenes in a row
were the same mood, the next spontaneous card should be the opposite.

## Techniques

- **The pivot**: start a scene comic, end it grim (the hilarious drunk is
  drunk because his daughter is missing) — or the reverse.
- **The mundane detail** in horror: the murderer's lunch, half-eaten.
- **Gallows humour from NPCs** who live in a terrible world: soldiers,
  gravediggers, plague doctors are the funniest people in any setting.
- **Honest villains**: give them a point. Players should hesitate once.
- **Victories that taste weird**: they saved the town; the town is
  ungrateful/terrified of them.
- **Specificity**: "a man" is nothing; "a man in a wedding coat three sizes
  too big" is a story.

## Adult content guidelines

- In scope: gore and body horror, profanity, crude and sexual humour,
  addiction, cruelty, religious horror, political violence, moral dilemmas
  without good answers, death of NPCs including sympathetic ones.
- Sex: implied or fade-to-black by default; Mug sets explicitness in
  `campaign.md → Lines & Veils`.
- Never sexualize minors. Never. Not as a joke, not as villainy shown on
  screen.
- Torture, sexual violence, child harm: default **veiled** (it happened,
  we don't show it) unless the table decides otherwise — and even then,
  sexual violence stays offscreen.
- Real-world groups aren't punchlines; in-world prejudice can exist and be
  shown as ugly.

## Safety tools (adults still use them)

- **Session 0**: set Lines (never) and Veils (offscreen) — record them.
- **X-card / "let's skip that"**: any player can tap out of content; no
  questions. I rewrite on the fly.
- **Open door**: players can step away.
- After a heavy scene: Mug can call a 5-minute break. I'll suggest one if
  a scene ran very dark.

## Pacing

- Open every session *in motion* — mid-situation, not "you wake up at the
  inn."
- Each session wants: one laugh-out-loud moment, one gut-punch, one "oh
  shit" discovery, one moment where a PC's personal story matters.
- End sessions on a cliffhanger or a quiet, ominous image.
- If energy drops: an NPC makes a decision, something attacks, or a clock
  ticks visibly. Never ask the players "what do you do?" to a void —
  give them something to push against.
- Combat that's decided but not finished: end it narratively.

## Sample voice

Bad: "You enter a tavern. It's busy."
Good: "The Gilded Ferret is busy the way a wound is busy. Someone's been
sick behind the hearth and the cook is fanning it with a ladle. On the
wall, under a mounted boar's head, a hand-painted sign reads NO
NECROMANCY, and beneath that, newer: AGAIN."
