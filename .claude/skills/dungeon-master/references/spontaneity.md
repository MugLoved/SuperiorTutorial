# Spontaneity Engine

Most of a session is scripted. These rules make each session still feel
unrepeatable. Spontaneous events are *offered* to Mug as optional cards —
never forced.

## When to roll

- **Scene transition**: roll d6. On a **1**, draw an event from the table
  matching the context. On a **6**, add a small flavour detail only.
- **Long travel / downtime**: one roll per in-game day (d6, 1–2 triggers).
- **Combat**: at the start of round 3, roll d6; on 1, a combat twist.
- **Players doing something nobody planned**: always consider a fitting
  card — reward creativity with world reaction.
- **Max 2 big spontaneous events per session.** Unused ones go to the
  "banked" list in the session log and can return later.

## Card format

```
🎲 SPONTANEOUS (optional) — <title>
What happens: 1–3 sentences.
Why it's fun: the hook (funny / dark / serious / all three).
Ties to: an existing thread, NPC or PC backstory if possible.
If ignored: what it becomes later.
```

Always try to **tie** a random event to something established. Random +
connected = feels like destiny. Random + unconnected = feels like noise.

## Rolling

Use real randomness: `shuf -i 1-20 -n 1`. Then remix the result — the
table is a seed, not a script. Never use the same entry twice in a
campaign; mark used entries in `campaign.md`.

## Road & wilderness (d20)

1. A dead horse in full harness, still warm. No rider. The saddlebags are
   full of love letters addressed to different people, all signed by the
   same person.
2. A hermit offers to read fortunes. Every fortune is accurate and petty.
3. A wagon of pilgrims singing a hymn whose lyrics, translated, are a
   recipe.
4. Wolves following at a distance for two days. They are not hungry. They
   are escorting the party *away* from something.
5. A tree with a noose and a sign: "Not today. Maybe tomorrow." The rope is
   new each morning.
6. A lost tax collector who is genuinely kind, terrified, and carrying 400
   gp of the poor's money.
7. A bridge troll unionized with other bridge trolls; there's a strike.
8. A shrine to a god nobody remembers. Praying there gives inspiration and
   the god's loneliness (WIS DC 12 or a night of weeping dreams).
9. Rain of frogs. Some are alive. One talks, but only insults.
10. Bandits who already got robbed by someone worse, asking for help.
11. A child's toy on the road, every mile, getting closer to a ruined house.
12. A duel between two old men over a woman who died forty years ago.
13. A travelling executioner looking for work, cheerful, with references.
14. Fog in which the party hears their own voices from a few minutes ago.
15. A merchant selling "cursed" items. Most are fine. One isn't. He doesn't
    know which.
16. A deserter from the army the party will soon meet, with information
    and a bounty on his head.
17. An enormous, harmless beast dying; scavengers circling; the beast is
    the last of its kind.
18. A cart overturned; the cargo is coffins; one knocks back.
19. A PC's backstory NPC, older and changed, walking the other way.
20. Perfect weather, a meadow, peace. Let them rest. Then note that
    something has been watching the whole time.

## Town & tavern (d20)

1. A bar fight breaks out over the correct way to cook a potato. Sides form.
2. A drunk wizard has accidentally polymorphed himself into a goose and is
   furious about it.
3. Public hanging today. The condemned asks one PC by name to speak for him.
   They've never met.
4. A bard sings a wildly inaccurate, extremely popular song about the
   party's last adventure.
5. Plague rumor; a neighbourhood gets walled off — with someone inside the
   party needs.
6. The innkeeper's marriage is collapsing loudly and both want the party as
   witnesses.
7. A guard asks for a bribe, apologizes, explains his kid is sick; it's true.
8. A cult recruiting in the square is offering free soup. It's good soup.
9. A pickpocket steals from a PC and then returns it because "you look
   like you need it more."
10. A funeral procession for a dog, attended by the entire watch.
11. A noble's assassination attempt fails in front of the party; they're
    the only witnesses and the obvious suspects.
12. Wanted posters with a terribly drawn face of a PC. Wrong crime.
13. A wedding needs one more guest for luck; refusal is an insult.
14. An old adventurer, legless, drunk, wants to hear their story and then
    tells them how his party died doing the exact same job.
15. A fire. Someone is screaming upstairs.
16. A shop going out of business; everything half price; the owner is
    leaving town *tonight* and won't say why.
17. Children playing "adventurers" — reenacting the party, badly, one
    "dies" dramatically.
18. A priest offers free healing, then a confession booth that is
    definitely being listened to.
19. Brothel madam (or equivalent) hires the party for a missing-person
    case nobody else will touch.
20. An NPC the party wronged is in the room. They haven't noticed yet.

## Dungeon & ruin (d20)

1. A previous adventuring party's corpses, with a journal whose last entry
   is about the room the party is in right now.
2. Goblins having a birthday party. Surprise, everyone.
3. A trap that already went off; the victim is alive, stuck, and chatty.
4. A mimic that's lonely, not hungry.
5. Graffiti in Common giving bad advice. Further in, graffiti correcting it.
6. The torch flames bend toward a wall. Hidden draft. Hidden door.
7. A ghost who doesn't know they're dead and asks the party to deliver rent.
8. Rival adventurers, better equipped, worse people.
9. A room that is perfectly clean. Dust everywhere else. Something tends it.
10. A monster's nest with young. What do you do with the young?
11. A collapsed tunnel splits the party for one scene.
12. An altar with an offering bowl and a note: "Take one."
13. Water rises slowly. Clock: 6 segments.
14. A prisoner in chains who swears they're innocent. They're not. But
    they're not what the party thinks either.
15. Bones arranged into a word in an unknown language. A PC knows it.
16. A sentient door with opinions about the party's choices.
17. The dungeon's "boss" is negotiating to sell the dungeon.
18. A magical echo of a massacre replays; DC 13 WIS or frightened.
19. Treasure that is real but stuck to the floor with a curse of greed:
    whoever touches it can't let go until they give something up.
20. Daylight through a crack — a secret exit, and the sound of the
    surface world going on normally, uncaring.

## Combat twists (d12)

1. Weather turns: wind (ranged disadv), rain (fire weak), lightning.
2. A third party arrives with its own agenda.
3. An enemy surrenders and begs; their friends kill them for it.
4. The floor/bridge/ship starts failing. Clock: 3 rounds.
5. An enemy recognises a PC: "*You.*"
6. Collateral: innocents in the line of fire.
7. A weapon breaks on a nat 1 — or is it the enemy's weapon on a nat 20?
8. The enemy leader is a coward and runs with the loot.
9. Fire spreads; smoke = lightly obscured next round, heavily after.
10. A beast nearby panics and charges through.
11. Enemy reinforcements are kids pressed into service.
12. Ridiculous moment: an enemy's pants fall down. Everyone gets one
    round of disadvantage from laughing (WIS DC 10).

## Loot with a story (d12)

1. A coin purse with a name embroidered — the owner's family wants it back.
2. A sword that whispers *thank you* each time it kills.
3. A ring that makes the wearer sneeze when someone lies nearby.
4. Bottle of wine labelled with the date of a PC's death. In 40 years.
5. A map with the party's current location marked "HERE THEY DIE". Old ink.
6. A potion that tastes like the drinker's childhood.
7. A book of terrible poetry that is also a spellbook, encrypted in rhyme.
8. An urn of ashes and a note: "please scatter at the sea."
9. Gloves that leave no fingerprints and ruin all handwriting.
10. A deed to a property. It's haunted. It's also very nice.
11. A tooth of a god. Very valuable. Something wants it back.
12. A letter of pardon with the name left blank.

## Weather & detail spice (d8)

1. First snow. 2. Unseasonal heat, flies. 3. Fog thick as milk. 4. Red
sunset, superstitious locals. 5. Eclipse in 3 days. 6. Storm far off,
coming. 7. Festival tonight — fireworks, crowds, pickpockets. 8. Total
silence: no birds.
